## What the route is

L-002 (Wirsching 2003, DCDS 9(3)) does NOT extend or use the Weak Covering Conjecture / `R_{j,k}`
(Chapter V) at all, contrary to this project's original assumption. It pursues positive
predecessor density via a completely different apparatus: Elka functions `e_l(k,a)` counting
Collatz-graph paths, Haar-measure averaging over 3-adic integers, a limiting Markov chain with
invariant density `phi`, and three new conjectures.

**Deep tractability scoping (2026-07-29)**: the whole Positive-Density proof reduces to exactly
three conjectures (Pos.Density <= (1) <= [Conj1] (2) <= (3) <= [Conj2] (4) <= [sec7] (5) =
[Conj3]). **Conjecture 3 is the load-bearing analytic gap and it does not live in Collatz**: it is
the near-0 asymptotics of the invariant density `phi`, a two-scale/refinement functional-equation
eigenfunction. Conjectures 1 and 2 are translation/uniformity-near-singularity gaps specific to
the construction, not secretly resolved and not finitely checkable. None of the three is WCC's
`S(t)`/rank-coupling obstruction in disguise: this is a genuinely independent hardness, not a
rephrasing.

**Recommendation, unchanged since 2026-07-29**: stays backlog, low-medium. Rule 1/comparative
advantage: the route uses none of this project's `R_{j,k}`/jstar-fast apparatus, and its core is

**Bridge to H-002 (Rule 8e, 2026-07-29)**: the Elka route is the equidistribution *strengthening*

## Conjecture 3, precisely

`phi` is the unique fixed point of `W3 phi(x) = (3/2) integral_{3x-2}^{3x} phi`: `supp phi =
[0,1]`, `integral phi = 1`, `C^infinity`, polynomial on each interval outside a Cantor set, and
satisfies `(2/3) phi'(t) = 3(phi(3t) - phi(3t-2))`, i.e. the `a=3, lambda=2/3` case of `lambda
phi'(t) = a(phi(at) - phi(at-a+1))`.

Conjecture 3 states `phi(z_l)/phi_0(z_l) -> c > 0` uniformly for `(z_l)` in a class corresponding
to `z_l ~ l*3^{-l}` (the "borderline speed" toward 0 that makes the section-7 quotient tend to a
positive limit), where `phi_0` is the explicit truncated-equation asymptotic solution (Berg-
Kruppel, eq. 9.6): `phi_0(t) = ((2*beta)^epsilon/sqrt(2*pi)) * exp(gamma*ln(t) + delta*ln(-ln t) -
beta*ln^2(t/(-ln t)))`.

## Primary-source verification chain (all read in full, not title-matched)

1. **Berg & Kruppel, "On the Solution of an Integral-Functional Equation with a Parameter," Z.
   Anal. Anwendungen 17(1) (1998), 159-181 (L-097)**: the actual primary source of Conjecture 3's
   premise. Section 9, Proposition 9.1, eq. (9.6) gives `g_0(t)`'s asymptotic. The authors
   themselves state only that they "expect" `phi ~ g_0` up to a bounded, bounded-away-from-zero
   factor — an informal expectation, never even formally conjectured with a proof attempt, and
   explicitly weaker than Wirsching's actual Conjecture 3.
2. **Berg & Kruppel, "Cantor Sets and Integral-Functional Equations," Z. Anal. Anwendungen 17(4)
   (1998), 997-1020 (L-096)**: sequel, gives exact polynomial formulas for `phi` on the Cantor-
   complement, no asymptotic content.
3. **Berg & Kruppel, "Series Expansions...," Results in Math. 41 (2002), 213-228 (L-098)**: purely
   series-expansion/spline content, no asymptotic-as-t-to-0 analysis.
4. **Volk, "Evaluation of the solution of an integral-functional equation," J. Comp. Appl. Math.
   174 (2005), 423-436 (L-099)**: purely computational (Taylor/Horner evaluation algorithm),
   exclusively for `a=2`, not `a=3`. Author corrected from an earlier misattribution to Berg-
   Kruppel (Rule 11 note: the error came from relying on an indirect summary, fixed once the PDF
   was read).

**Net: Conjecture 3 is a genuine, still-open gap, never claimed closed even by its own
originating paper.** This is a primary-source-verified negative, not a search-coverage gap.

## The "analogous framings" search (2026-07-31): phi = h_3, and the ensuing literature exhaustion

At the researcher's request to search disguised/analogous versions of this project's objects
under unrelated names (see `notes/H-003.md` for the full three-object search), Codex identified
that `phi` is not merely analogous to but literally (up to translation) Rvachev's classical
"atomic function" `h_3`: `phi(x) = h_3(x-1/2)`. **Independently verified** by direct symbolic
substitution into the already-established functional equation (`psi(y):=phi(y+1/2)` satisfies
exactly Rvachev's equation at `a=3`), not taken on faith.

This triggered a full read of the entire published Kabaya/Iri/Moriguti/Rvachev/Derfel line
connected to this identity, described below. **All of it is now read, not sampled.**

- **Kabaya & Iri, "Sum of Uniformly Distributed Random Variables and a Family of Nonanalytic
  C^infinity-Functions," Japan J. Appl. Math. 4 (1987), 1-22 (L-105)**: read in full (22pp).
  Confirms `phi` is exactly this paper's `alpha=2/3` family member (a second, independent
  parametrization matching the `h_3` identity: `X_n = alpha*[U_n+(1-alpha)U_{n-1}+...]`, matching
  via `1-alpha=1/3`). Proves existence/smoothness and Cantor-set nonanalyticity (Theorems 1-2):
  for `alpha>1/2` (our case), the density is polynomial on each Cantor-complement interval,
  independently cross-checking L-096's finding via a wholly different 1987 derivation.
  **Contains no boundary/near-0 asymptotic result of any kind.**
- **Kabaya-Imai & Iri, "On Operators Defining a Family of Nonanalytic C^infinity-Functions,"
  Japan J. Appl. Math. 5 (1988), 333-365 (L-108)**: read in full (33pp), the 1987 paper's own
  sequel. Full spectral theory of the operator `Phi` (eigenvalues `(1-alpha)^n`, eigenvectors
  `p_infinity^(n)` = derivatives of `phi`, dual operator `Phi*` with orthogonal polynomial
  eigenvectors `q_n`). **No near-0 boundary asymptotic for `phi` itself.** Section 7.2 records an
  unproven numerical conjecture that `q_n(x)` converges to `cos/sin(pi*x/alpha)` as `n ->
  infinity` — resolved by the next paper.
- **Moriguti, Iri & Kabaya-Imai, "On Asymptotic Properties of the Eigenfunctions of a Linear
  Operator," Japan J. Appl. Math. 7 (1990), 203-229 (L-109)**: given the title, this was the
  round's top candidate to close Conjecture 3 outright. Read in full (27pp). **Confirmed real,
  rigorous theorem (generating-function/Jacobi-theta technique), confirmed NOT applicable**: it
  proves `q_n(x) -> sin/cos(pi*x/alpha)` as `n -> infinity` (the DUAL family's DEGREE INDEX, for
  fixed `x`), linear rate `max{2^-n, (1-alpha)^n}`. This is an index asymptotic, not the spatial
  (`x -> 0`) asymptotic Conjecture 3 needs — the same mismatch pattern as L-106 (Brysina-
  Makarichev): a title promising "asymptotic properties of eigenfunctions" means convergence in
  the family/degree index, not decay near a boundary point.
- **Derfel, "Functional-Differential and Functional Equations with Rescaling," Operator Theory:
  Advances and Applications 80 (1995), 100-111 (L-107)**: read in full (12pp). **Major
  contextualization finding**: Theorem 1 (from the author's 1977 PhD thesis) proves that
  `exp(-gamma*ln^2(1+|t|))` (matching Berg-Kruppel's `g_0` form exactly) is the GENERIC asymptotic
  rate for the entire class of rescaled functional-differential equations (the "Kato-McLeod"
  theory, after Kato & McLeod, Bull. AMS 77 (1971), 891-937), not something ad hoc to Wirsching's
  equation. Theorem 2 shows the bound is essentially sharp for the pure-dilation sub-case. Section
  3 gives a general existence theorem (ref. [Der6]) for compactly-supported solutions of exactly
  `phi`'s equation type, independently confirming the `h_3` identity from a third tradition. This
  survey's own bibliography is what led to finding L-109.
- **Rvachev, "Compactly Supported Solutions of Functional-Differential Equations and Their
  Applications," Russian Math. Surveys 45:1 (1990), 87-120 (L-111)**: the comprehensive,
  authoritative 1990 survey of the entire Soviet/Ukrainian atomic-function tradition. Read in full
  (34pp). **Confirmed: entirely scoped to `a=2` (`up(x)`) and to approximation-theoretic questions
  (generalized Taylor series, spline/trigonometric approximation, interpolation-node theorems).
  No near-0/boundary asymptotic question appears anywhere, including in the paper's own explicit
  "unsolved problems" section.** The field's own most authoritative stocktaking did not consider
  Conjecture-3-type asymptotics a live question, reinforcing from the Soviet/approximation-theory
  side what Berg-Kruppel's papers already showed from the German side.
- **Brysina & Makarichev, "On the asymptotics of the generalized Fup-functions," Adv. Pure Appl.
  Math. 5 (2014), 131-138 (L-106)**: abstract-level read only (paywalled). Same index-vs-spatial
  mismatch: "asymptotics" here means convergence of a family index `n -> infinity`, not near-0
  decay of a fixed function.

**Self-decomposable/stable-law techniques ruled out definitively (not just deprioritized)**:
`phi`'s characteristic function has zeros (sinc-product form `prod_r sin(xi/3^r)/(xi/3^r)`), but
every infinitely divisible or self-decomposable characteristic function is nowhere-zero by the
Levy-Khintchine representation. ~0% confidence, confirmed via the zero-set argument alone.

**A separate, unproven, checkable mechanism for the ratio question (not yet tested)**: taking logs
of `phi`'s Laplace transform gives a base-3 harmonic sum (Flajolet-Gourdon-Dumas sense); such sums
generically produce a log-periodic correction under Mellin analysis (de Bruijn 1948 precedent),
explaining why Berg-Kruppel only claim boundedness. Codex derived that the specific sequence
`t_l ~ l*3^{-l}` may "phase-lock" this multiplier (saddle-point inversion puts `log_3(s(t_l))` at
a fixed phase as `l -> infinity` along that sequence). Cheapest test if picked up: compute
`phi(t_l)/g_0(t_l)` numerically at several `l`. **Attempted and deferred, not run**: this is not
actually cheap — it needs either Berg-Kruppel's exact eq. (9.6) constants (not yet extracted with
sufficient precision) or a from-scratch high-precision evaluator for `phi` near `t=0`, the exact
numerically delicate problem Volk's whole paper (L-099) solved only for `a=2`. Would need its own
properly scoped experiment.
