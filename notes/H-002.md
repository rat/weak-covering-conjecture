## What "the bridge" actually requires, worked out from scratch

WCC says: for l terms and a sufficiently large budget k, R_{l-1,k} (Wirsching's bounded,
finite-budget object) covers all of `(Z/3^l Z)*`. beta=1 says: `c_l = inf_b P(Syrac=b) =
3^{-l+o(l)}`, where the probability is over Tao's i.i.d. Geom(2) sample `u_1,...,u_l` (using `u`
to avoid clashing with Wirsching's `a_i` exponent notation), each `u_m >= 1`,
`P(u_1=v_1,...,u_l=v_l) = 2^{-(v_1+...+v_l)}`.

The natural bridge argument: if residue `b` is hit by *some* admissible Wirsching tuple, that
tuple corresponds (via the already-confirmed object-identity reindexing) to *some* specific
sample `(u_1,...,u_l)` with total `N := u_1+...+u_l`. That one sample realizes `Syrac=b` with
probability `2^{-N}`, giving a valid lower bound `P(Syrac=b) >= 2^{-N}`. To get `c_l >=
3^{-l+o(l)}` from this, every covered residue needs *some* representing sample with cost
`N <= l*log_2(3) + o(l)`.

This looks mechanical at first glance (WCC already says a representing tuple exists once the
budget is large enough, and `j*(l)` grows like `l*log_4(3) = l*log_2(3)/2 + o(l)`, well within
budget). **It is not mechanical**, for a reason that only shows up when relating Wirsching's
budget parameter to Tao's actual sample cost `N`, demonstrated concretely below.

## The concrete gap, demonstrated on the l=2 baseline, not just asserted

Re-derived the exact correspondence between a Wirsching tuple `(a_0,...,a_{l-1})` and the
original sample `(u_1,...,u_l)`, from `literature/notes/L-003.md`'s already-confirmed reindexing
(`g_i := b_{l-1}-b_i`, `b_i := u_{l-i}+...+u_l`). For `l=2`: `b_0=u_2`, `b_1=u_1+u_2=N`, so
`a_0=g_0=b_1-b_0=u_1` and `a_1=g_1=b_1-b_1=0`, **forced to exactly 0** — a genuine 2-step i.i.d.
Geom(2) sample can only ever produce a Wirsching tuple whose *second* (smaller) exponent is 0.

But this project's own verified `R_{1,2}` (`notes/H-001.md`'s l=2,j=2 checkpoint) contains tuples
with a nonzero second exponent. Recomputed directly just now to confirm, not from memory:

```
(1,0) -> 5      (2,0) -> 7      (2,1) -> 1
(3,0) -> 2      (3,1) -> 5      (3,2) -> 2
```

Three of the six tuples (`(2,1)`, `(3,1)`, `(3,2)`) have `a_1 != 0` and therefore **cannot
correspond to any genuine 2-step Geom(2) sample** under the direct reindexing above. These are
half of `R_{1,2}`'s tuples, contributing to 3 of its 4 covered residues — not a corner case.

**What this means, stated precisely**: Wirsching's finite-budget `R_{j,k}` (fixed term-count `l`,
budget `k`) is *not* simply "the set of values reachable by exactly-`l`-step Geom(2) samples."
The extra tuples correspond to samples of a *different* underlying structure — consistent with
H-004's finding tonight that the general back-tracing formalism mixes a variable number of `T0`
(doubling) and `T1` (odd) moves, not a fixed `l`-step-only Geom(2) walk. The "one representing
sample, cost `2^{-N}`" argument needs `N` computed for the *correct* underlying sample structure,
which is not simply `a_0` (Wirsching's largest exponent) read off the bounded tuple — that
identification, implicitly assumed by treating "budget in Wirsching's sense" and "cost in Tao's
sense" as the same scale, is exactly the unproven step. `N` and the budget-scale quantity likely
still track each other closely (both are bounded by essentially the same `j*(l)`-order
quantity), but "closely" is not "exactly," and the entropy-count argument needs the exact
relationship, not an order-of-magnitude one, to conclude `c_l = 3^{-l+o(l)}` rather than a
weaker `c_l >= 3^{-l-o(l)*C}` for some unpinned constant `C` — the gap between these is exactly
the gap between beta=1 and a weaker density statement.

## Connection to H-003's non-uniformity finding: a concrete, testable next step

`notes/H-003.md`'s fourth/fifth dated sections found, empirically, that at `j=j*(l)` the
distribution of tuple-counts per residue is extremely non-uniform: the rarest residue is hit by
as few as 1 tuple, the most common by hundreds (l=11: min=4, max=401). This is directly relevant
here: **the rarest residue's `c_l` lower bound is determined entirely by that one tuple's actual
Tao-cost `N`**, not by `a_0` or by the tuple count. If that single tuple has an unusually large
`N` relative to `l*log_2(3)`, `c_l` could be smaller than beta=1 predicts even though the residue
is technically "covered" in Wirsching's sense.

**Concrete next step, not yet done, flagged rather than attempted here** (this task's scope was
to narrow the gap, not close it): for a residue with `min_count=1` at `j=j*(l)`, invert the
Wirsching tuple back to its actual underlying `(u_1,...,u_l)` sample (via the general back-tracing
correspondence H-004 established, not the naive `l=2`-only reindexing above, now known to be
incomplete) and compute its real cost `N`. Compare `N` against `l*log_2(3)`. If `N` tracks
tightly, the entropy-count bridge likely holds with a more careful derivation. If `N` is
significantly larger for the rarest residues, that is a genuine, specific obstruction to beta=1
as currently stated, not just an unproven technicality.
