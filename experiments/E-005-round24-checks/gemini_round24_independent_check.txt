## Background definitions

j*(l) := smallest j such that R_{j-1,j} covers (Z/3^l Z)^* mod 3^l. Known exactly for l=1..23.
e(l) := j*(l) - l*log_4(3). Best proven upper bound: j*(l) <= (119/104)*l + O(1) (an exact
mean-payoff-game solve). Best proven lower bound: e(l) >= (1/4)*log_2(l) - O(1) (elementary
pigeonhole). This project's own value = sum_{i=1}^{l} 2^{s_i} 3^{i-1} for chosen decreasing
exponents s_1>...>s_l, is proven equal to a unit multiple of Tao's Syracuse random variable
Syrac(Z/3^n Z) := sum_{m=1}^{n} 3^{m-1} 2^{-a_[1,m]}, a_j iid Geom(2), P(a_j=k)=2^{-k}, k>=1,

## ITEM 2: a preliminary numerical pattern (needs a genuinely skeptical read)

Define q_{l,r} := (2*3^{r-1}) * min_b p_{l,r}(b), where p_{l,r}(b) is the fraction of the exact

congruent to b mod 3^r (a "shallow cylinder", r<l). Surjectivity of the covering property gives
the exact inequality (domain size)/(2*3^{l-1}) >= q_{l,r}^{-1}, so a bound on how small q_{l,r}
can be translates directly into a lower bound on j*(l) (hence on e(l)).

Computed q_{l,r} EXACTLY (not estimated) for every l=4..23 (all currently known exact j*(l)
values) and r up to 12, via a reduced-modulus rerun of an already-validated rotation-DP (the
