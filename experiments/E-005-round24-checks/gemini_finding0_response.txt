### 2. Methodological Scrutiny: Why $(j^*(l) - l)$ Staying in $\{2..5\}$ is a Misleading Signal

The inference that "$\delta(l) := j^*(l) - l$ staying flat implies $e(l)$ is asymptotically linear" suffers from **three severe structural/statistical flaws**:

#### A. The Integer Discretization Trap (Resolution Limit)
$j^*(l)$ is strictly integer-valued. Consider what happens if the true asymptotic growth of $\delta(l)$ is sub-linear (e.g., $\delta(l) \sim c \sqrt{l}$ or $c \log l$):
* If $\delta(l) = 4 + 0.2 \log_2(l/16)$:
  * At $l=16$: $\delta(16) = 4.0 \rightarrow \text{rounded } 4$
  * At $l=23$: $\delta(23) = 4 + 0.2 \times 0.52 = 4.10 \rightarrow \text{rounded } 4$
* If $\delta(l) = 3 + 0.2 \sqrt{l}$:
  * At $l=16$: $\delta(16) = 3 + 0.8 = 3.8 \rightarrow \text{rounded } 4$
  * At $l=23$: $\delta(23) = 3 + 0.95 = 3.95 \rightarrow \text{rounded } 4$

Over the range $l \in [16, 23]$, **any sub-linear growth term with a modest constant varies by less than 0.5 integer units.** An integer-valued sequence *must* display flat plateaus over such ranges regardless of whether the underlying continuous regime is $O(1)$, $O(\log l)$, or $O(\sqrt{l})$. Interpreting an 8-point integer plateau as proof of $O(1)$ boundedness is a classic resolution artifact.

#### B. The Default Step Mechanics of $j^*(l)$ ($4 > 3$)
When moving from $l$ to $l+1$, the target set size expands by a factor of $3$ ($|(\mathbb{Z}/3^{l+1}\mathbb{Z})^*| = 3 \cdot |(\mathbb{Z}/3^l \mathbb{Z})^*|$). Incrementing $j \to j+1$ expands the tuple search space by a factor of roughly $4$. 
Because $4 > 3$, the default increment required to maintain coverage when $l$ increases by 1 is **$\Delta j = 1$**.
Notice what $\Delta j = 1$ implies for $\delta(l)$:
$$\delta(l+1) - \delta(l) = (j^*(l+1) - (l+1)) - (j^*(l) - l) = j^*(l+1) - j^*(l) - 1$$
If $j^*(l+1) = j^*(l) + 1$, then $\delta(l+1) - \delta(l) = 0$ **by definition**.
Thus, $\delta(l)$ remaining constant over $l=16..23$ simply reflects that $j^*(l)$ stepped by 1 at each step. That is the baseline discrete behavior of the system, **not** positive evidence of an asymptotic limit. $\delta(l)$ only changes when $j^*(l)$ jumps by 0 or by $\ge 2$.

#### C. Structural Lower Bound Bottleneck at Small $l$
For $j < l$, the expression $\sum_{i=0}^j 2^{a_i} 3^i$ contains no powers of 3 at or above $3^l$. To cover $(\mathbb{Z}/3^l \mathbb{Z})^*$, lower powers of 3 must propagate carry digits all the way up into the higher 3-adic positions. At small $l$ ($l \le 23$), this carry-propagation constraint enforces a hard local structural floor of $j^*(l) \ge l$. 
At $l=23$, the tuple space size ($\approx 1.9 \times 10^{15}$) is already $30,000\times$ larger than the target group size ($6.27 \times 10^{10}$). Global volume is nowhere near binding; local 3-adic digit coverage is the sole bottleneck. Observing $j^*(l) \approx l$ at small $l$ tells you about local carry constraints, not about asymptotic volume scaling.
