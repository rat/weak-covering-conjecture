## Central finding: this paper does NOT extend or use the Weak Covering Conjecture / R_{j,k}

This is the important correction to the assumption in this project's `literature/INDEX.md`
entry going in ("likely the paper most likely to contain any further numerical/theoretical
development of the covering conjecture beyond the 1998 book"). That assumption was wrong.
Grepped the full text for "covering", "R_{j,k}"-style notation, and "weak covering": no hits.
Every citation back to the 1998 book (L-001, cited here as [4]) is to Chapters II, III, and IV
(general Collatz-graph structure, "balls in constrained urns," a functional differential
equation), never to Chapter V (where Corollary/Notation 1.8's R_{j,k} and Conjecture 3.9's Weak
Covering Conjecture actually live). Wirsching's own follow-up work on predecessor density took
a **different technical route entirely**, not an extension of the covering-conjecture chapter
this project is built around.

## What the paper actually does

Same overarching goal as the 1998 book's Reduction Theorem 3.10 (uniform positive predecessor
density for the 3n+1 map), pursued via a different apparatus:

- **Elka functions**: `e_l(k,a) := |E_{l,k}(a)|`, counting paths of length `l+k` in the Collatz
  graph from some `b` back to `a` using exactly `l` steps of `T1` (the `(3n+1)/2` branch) and
  `k` steps of `T0` (the `/2` branch). Key property (cited from the 1998 book, Section II.4, not
  Chapter V): `a == b mod 3^l` implies `e_l(k,a) = e_l(k,b)` for all k, which lets Elka functions
  be extended to arguments in `Z_3` (3-adic integers) rather than just `N`.
- Averages of Elka functions over the Haar measure on the unit group `Z_3^x` of 3-adic integers,
  a sequence of integral-preserving operators `S_l` on `C(X)` shown to converge to a limiting
  operator `S_infinity`, whose essential part defines a Markov chain on `[0,1]` with an invariant
  density `phi`.
- Three new conjectures (Conjecture 1, 2, 3, none named "covering" and none stated in terms of
  `R_{j,k}`) about growth-rate conditions on `phi` and related quantities, explicitly flagged in
  the abstract as "three unbridged gaps in the argument," needed to complete the positive-density
  proof via this route.

No table of numeric values (of `j*(l)`, `e_l(k,a)`, or anything else resembling this project's
verified l=1..23 table) appears anywhere in the paper. This is a purely analytic/ergodic-theory
argument, not a computational one.

### The logical spine (verified against the primary source)

The paper builds a chain of five conditions and proves/conjectures each link. Reading the
implications in the direction they are used (each condition is implied by the next one down):

```
Positive Density  <==  (?1)   Theorem 1        PROVEN
      (?1)        <==  (?2)   Conjecture 1     CONJECTURAL   <- gap 1
      (?2)        <==  (?3)   Theorem 2        PROVEN
      (?3)        <==  (?4)   Conjecture 2     CONJECTURAL   <- gap 2
      (?4)        <==  (?5)   section 7 calc   PROVEN
      (?5)               Conjecture 3          CONJECTURAL   <- gap 3
```

So the "three unbridged gaps" of the abstract are exactly Conjectures 1, 2, 3. Everything else
(Theorems 1-3, the operator-convergence Theorem 3, the section-7 final calculation) is proven.
The whole proof of Positive Density reduces to these three conjectures and nothing else.

### What the three conjectures actually claim

**Conjecture 1  ((?2) => (?1)).** A pointwise lower bound on the *generators* transfers to the
*Elka functions* themselves. Precisely: if `g_l(k_l,a) >= mu1 * gbar_l(k_l)` uniformly for
sequences `(k_l)` in the class `A_delta1 = {(k_l): |l - k_l| <= delta1*sqrt(l)}` (so `k_l` within
`sqrt(l)` of `l`, i.e. roughly equal counts of T0 and T1 steps), then the same holds for Elka
functions, `e_l(k_l,a) >= mu * ebar_l(k_l)`. The mechanism is the convolution identity
`e_l = p_l * g_l` (eq 2.3), where `p_l(m)` is the number of ways to pay `m` in coins of values
`c_0 = 1, c_j = 2*3^{j-1}` (a partition / coin-change count). The gap: the convolution kernel
`p_l` is `a`-independent, and one must show it does not wash out the `a`-dependent lower bound on
`g_l`. Combinatorial, not analytic.

**Conjecture 2  ((?4) => (?3)).** The ratio of the *limiting* operator applied to two fixed
characteristic functions controls the *actual* normalized generators. With `chi_0 = 1` on
`[0, 2/3]` and `chi_1 = 1` on `[1/3, 1]`, if `(W3^l chi_1)(x_l) / (W3^l chi_0)(x_l) >= mu`
uniformly for `(x_l)` in the translated class `Atilde_delta`, then the normalized generators
satisfy the corresponding pointwise-vs-Haar-average bound `(?3)`. Here `W3 f(x) = (3/2)
integral_{3x-2}^{3x} f` is the essential part of the limiting operator `S_infinity`, and
`S_l -> S_infinity` is proven (Theorem 3, strong operator topology, uniform on equicontinuous
families). The gap: Theorem 3 gives convergence of the operators, but Conjecture 2 needs the
*ratio* to be controlled along a sequence `x_l -> 0` that marches toward the singular boundary
`x=0` as `l` grows. Strong-operator convergence does not deliver uniformity along such a moving
evaluation point near the singularity. This is a uniformity-near-a-singularity gap.

**Conjecture 3  ((?5), the load-bearing one).** An asymptotic property of the invariant density
`phi`. `phi` is the unique fixed point of `W3` (Corollary 7): `supp phi = [0,1]`,
`integral phi = 1`, `W3 phi = phi`; it is `C^infinity`, polynomial on each interval outside the
Cantor set, and satisfies the integral-functional equation `(2/3) phi'(t) = 3(phi(3t) -
phi(3t-2))`, i.e. the `a=3, lambda=2/3` case of `lambda*phi'(t) = a(phi(at) - phi(at-a+1))`.
Conjecture 3 states `phi(z_l)/phi_0(z_l) -> c > 0` uniformly for `(z_l)` in a class
`Atilde_delta5`, where `phi_0` is the *explicit* asymptotic solution of the **truncated**
equation `lambda*g'(t) = a*g(at)`, found by Berg-Kruppel [1] via Laplace transform + saddle
point (eq 7.11):
`phi_0(t) = ((2*beta)^epsilon / sqrt(2*pi)) * exp(gamma*ln t + delta*ln(-ln t) - beta*ln^2(t/(-ln t)))`.
The class `Atilde_delta5` corresponds to `z_l ~ l * 3^{-l}` (the "borderline speed" toward 0 that
makes the section-7 quotient tend to a positive limit). Berg-Kruppel's section 9 "suggests"
this is true, but note carefully: B-K themselves only *argued for* (did not prove) `phi ~ g_0`
up to a bounded, bounded-away-from-zero factor. Conjecture 3 asks for the stronger statement
that the ratio *converges* to a constant along the borderline class. So this is genuinely open
analysis, not a settled fact being cited.

### Tractability, conjecture by conjecture

**Conjecture 3 is the analytic heart, and it does not live in "Collatz" at all.** The object
`phi` is the invariant density of a two-scale / refinement-type functional equation; its
near-0 asymptotics are exactly the subject of a substantial, active analysis literature under
terminology an "Elka function" or "3n+1" search cannot reach: two-scale difference equations,
de Rham's singular function, Appell polynomials, Holder exponents of refinement-equation
solutions. Berg and Kruppel continued precisely this line after their 1998 paper [1]:
- L. Berg, M. Kruppel, "Eigenfunctions of two-scale difference equations and Appell polynomials,"
  Z. Anal. Anwendungen 20 (2001) 457-488.
- L. Berg, M. Kruppel, "Eigenfunctions of two-scale difference equations with rational symbol,"
  Result. Math. 44 (2003) 226-241.
- "De Rham's Singular Function, Two-Scale Difference Equations and Appell Polynomials"
  (Results in Mathematics), which estimates de Rham's function both ways and pins its exact
  Holder exponent.
(bibliographic details surfaced via web/Semantic-Scholar search 2026-07-29, NOT yet verified
against the primary sources; flagged unverified per Rule 11 until pulled and read.) This is the
single most useful scoping finding: Conjecture 3's content may be fully or partly settled in this
functional-equation literature, and the earlier negative "Elka function" search (which returned
nothing because the phrase is idiosyncratic to this one Collatz paper) structurally could not
have found it.

**2026-07-31 update: the primary source has now actually been pulled and read (Rule 11), not left
unverified.** Berg & Kruppel's foundational paper, "On the Solution of an Integral-Functional
Equation with a Parameter", Z. Anal. Anwendungen 17(1) (1998), 159-181, read in full (this is the
specific paper the `eq 7.11` / `phi_0` formula above comes from; its own internal section/equation
numbers differ slightly from the labels used earlier in this note, but the content matches
exactly: Section 9 "The truncated equation", Proposition 9.1, eq. (9.6), gives `g_0(t)`'s
asymptotic as `t -> +0` via a Laplace-transform saddle-point computation). The paper's own final
paragraph, checked directly rather than paraphrased: "we expect that `phi` has an asymptotic
behaviour as `g_0` in (9.6) up to a factor which is both bounded and bounded away from zero." This
is an informal expectation stated by the authors themselves, not a theorem and not even a formally
posed conjecture with an attempted proof, and it is explicitly WEAKER than what Wirsching's
Conjecture 3 needs (a stated, provable ratio *convergence* to a specific constant along the
borderline class `z_l ~ l*3^{-l}`, not just boundedness above and below). A second, EARLIER-in-time
sibling paper in the same 1998 journal issue, "Cantor Sets and Integral-Functional Equations"
(pp. 997-1020, immediately following Wirsching's own "Balls in Constrained Urns" paper at
pp. 979-996 in the same volume), was also read in full: it gives exact closed-form polynomial
representations of `phi` on the Cantor-set complement (full Lebesgue measure), useful structural
context, but contains no asymptotic/saddle-point analysis and does not touch Conjecture 3 either.
Two further, later Berg-Kruppel follow-ups were located (2002, "Series Expansions for the Solution
of an Integral-Functional Equation with a Parameter", Results in Math. 41, 213-228; ~2004,
"Evaluation of the solution of an integral-functional equation", J. Comp. Appl. Math.), both
paywalled and not read in full; their abstracts describe spline expansions and a numerical
evaluation algorithm respectively, neither obviously the sharpened asymptotic-convergence proof
Conjecture 3 would need, but this is not independently confirmed without the full text. **Net
effect: this is now a genuinely deeper, primary-source-verified confirmation that Conjecture 3
remains open** -- not merely a title-matching search that failed to find a resolution, but a
direct reading of the paper that STATES the open premise, showing its own authors never claimed
more than a bounded (not convergent) asymptotic relationship. See literature/INDEX.md L-096
through L-099 for the full citation records.

**Same-day follow-up: the researcher obtained and supplied L-098 and L-099 directly, both now read

Conjecture 3 is also *cheaply checkable numerically*: `phi` is a 1-D density,
`W3` is a plain averaging operator that contracts at rate 1/2 (Corollary 8), so iterating any
start density to `phi` at high precision is trivial (seconds, kilobytes of memory), and one can
then compute `phi(z_l)/phi_0(z_l)` along `z_l ~ l*3^{-l}` directly. Caveat: numerics can only
support, never prove, a uniform-limit statement, and the conjecture is already believed true.

**Conjectures 1 and 2 are "translation" gaps, not the analytic core, and are not secretly
resolved elsewhere.** Each is a specific uniformity-along-a-class statement tied to this exact
construction (the `p_l` convolution for Conj 1; the `S_l -> W3` operator limit near the boundary
for Conj 2). Neither is a rephrasing of a known theorem, and neither is meaningfully checkable by
finite computation: a uniform lower bound over an infinite class of sequences approaching a
singularity can be refuted numerically but never confirmed.

**Is any of the three secretly as hard as WCC itself, just rephrased? No.** They are different
mathematics. WCC / j*(l) / H-003 is stuck on a single obstruction, the exponential sum `S(t)`
over the rank-coupled index domain (twenty rounds, Fourier-analytic). The Elka route's hard core
is refinement-equation asymptotics (Conj 3) plus operator-limit uniformity (Conj 2) plus a
combinatorial convolution transfer (Conj 1). None of these is `S(t)` in disguise. That is
actually a point *in the route's favor* as an independent path: its hardness is a kind this
project has not already spent twenty rounds exhausting. The cost is that closing all three at
once equals proving Positive Density, which Wirsching himself calls "beyond the reach of methods
applied so far to 3n+1 dynamics."

### Bridge to this project's own R_{j,k} / j*(l) machinery, and to H-002

There is a real conceptual relationship, and it was worth the second read to pin it down
precisely rather than leaving the first pass's flat "low relevance."

The Elka function `e_l(k,a)` counts back-tracing paths landing on residue `a` mod `3^l` (property
1.2: `a == b mod 3^l => e_l(k,a) = e_l(k,b)`). This is the *same object family* as `R_{j,k}`:
H-004 already established `R_{J-1,J} = R(F_{J,J})` = the restricted Wirsching back-tracing image =
Tao's Syracuse set. So:

- **Covering (WCC, j*(l)) is the positivity/support question for `e_l`.** `R_{j,k}` covers `Z*_3`
  mod `3^l` iff every unit residue `a` is hit by some path, i.e. `e_l(k,a) > 0` for all units `a`
  (at the appropriate budget). j*(l) is the threshold where the support of `e_l(k, .)` fills the
  unit group.
- **The Elka density route asks for much more than positivity: a uniform lower bound relative to
  the average.** `(?1)` needs `e_l(k_l,a) >= mu * ebar_l(k_l)` uniformly, i.e. *equidistribution*
  (every residue hit a constant fraction of the average number of times), not merely coverage.
  Equidistribution strictly implies covering; the converse is false. So the Elka route is a
  strict *strengthening* of the WCC covering question, pursued by disjoint techniques.

**Flag, per Rule 8e: a genuine thematic bridge to H-002 (the entropy-count / Tao beta=1 gap).**
The Haar average of the Elka function has a clean closed form (eq 1.4):
`ebar_l(k) = C(k+l, k) / (2*3^{l-1})`. That is exactly an "expected count = total Collatz words /
number of unit residues" quantity: `sum_{a} e_l(k,a) = C(k+l,k)` words, divided over the
`2*3^{l-1}` units. This is the *same* expected-count denominator that underlies the entropy-count
/ beta=1 heuristic H-002 is trying to formalize. And the open part of both is identically shaped:
- H-002's gap is the pointwise-vs-average discrepancy `N_min` (the naive "covering tuple exists
  => probability lower bound" fails because the per-residue count deviates from the average; see
  H-002 row, "fails for 3 of R_{1,2}'s 6 tuples at l=2").
- Conjecture 1's gap is `g_l(k_l,a) >= mu1 * gbar_l(k_l)`: the same pointwise-per-residue count
  vs its Haar average.

So Wirsching's Elka apparatus is an *independent formalization of the same pointwise-vs-average
problem H-002 is stuck on*, and his eq (1.4) average is essentially H-002's denominator. This is
NOT a formal reduction (resolving Conjecture 1 or 3 would not automatically resolve H-002, and
vice versa), but it identifies a concrete candidate *source of the fluctuation control H-002
lacks*: the invariant density `phi` and the Berg-Kruppel asymptotics describe, in the `l -> inf`
limit, exactly how the per-residue generator counts distribute around their average. If that
asymptotic is pinned down (Conjecture 3 / the functional-equation literature), it is the natural
place to look for a principled handle on H-002's `N_min` fluctuation, which twenty rounds of the
Fourier/`S(t)` line have not supplied. Logged on both H-006 and H-002 in HYPOTHESES.md.
