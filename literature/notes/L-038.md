# L-038: M. E. Spencer, "Finite Block Exhaustion and Rooted Occupancy in the Inverse Collatz System"

Local archive, `literature/papers/022_Finite-Block-Exhaustion-Rooted-Occupancy-Inverse-Collatz.pdf`,
undated preprint, venue unclear (not a recognized peer-reviewed venue). Read in full for H-007.

## What it claims

A complete, unconditional proof of the full Collatz conjecture (Theorem 16.2), via an "exhaustion
of alternatives" argument on the inverse odd Collatz system.

## The real structural gap found

Sections 4-5 (the local affine dyadic atlas, valuation slices) are standard, correct number
theory. Section 6 introduces a **purely abstract** ternary occupancy counter: words over an
alphabet `{T, C0, C1}`, `W_J = A3^J`, with trivially-true counts `|W_J|=3^J`, `|C_J|=2^J`,
`|O_J|=3^J-2^J` (just counting an alphabet, no connection yet to actual integers or actual
inverse-Collatz paths). Section 9 separately defines actual admissible inverse words
`K=(k_0,...,k_{j-1})` and their affine action `F_K(n)`.

**The paper never rigorously defines or proves a bijection between these two objects.** Section 12
("Scale Matching and Finite Occupancy") asserts `|O_J| = |P_J|` (the abstract word count equals
the count of real primitive residues) and then, via Proposition 11.1 ("Disjoint Primitive
Placement", itself argued only in prose) and this equal-cardinality claim, concludes via a
pigeonhole-style argument (injective + equal-size finite sets => surjective) that "rooted
placement" covers every primitive residue (Corollary 12.3). But "rooted placement" - the actual
map from real admissible words to real residues - is never explicitly defined anywhere in the
paper as a function of the *abstract* `W_J`/`O_J` from Section 6; the two pieces of machinery
(Section 6's alphabet-counting and Section 9's real affine action) are introduced independently
and then silently identified. This is exactly the kind of equivocation-between-an-abstract-model-
and-the-real-object error that plagues this genre of paper: the easy part (counting an alphabet)
gets a trivial correct proof; the hard part (does this abstract count actually correspond to real,
distinct, exhaustive coverage of real residues) is asserted, not proven.

Consistent with this: Section 17's "machine-checked" Lean lemmas are *all* for the trivial
combinatorial identities (`card_word3`, `card_carried3`, `card_occupied3`, GCD facts) - none of
them touch the actual load-bearing step (the abstract-to-concrete identification in Section 12).
Formalizing the easy lemmas while leaving the hard, actually-doubtful step in informal prose is a
real warning sign, not proof of rigor.

## Relevance to H-003

None found. The paper never engages exponential sums, characters, Fourier analysis, or any
rank/position-weighted combinatorial structure - it's a different kind of argument (an exhaustion-
of-alternatives combinatorial proof attempt) aimed at a different, broader question (full Collatz
convergence, not this project's specific `j*(l)`/`R_{j,k}` covering rate).

## Credibility verdict

Not credible as a full proof of the Collatz conjecture. A genuine complete solution to a problem
this famous, from an unreviewed, self-published, single-author source, is already an extraordinary
claim; the specific gap identified above (a real, checkable flaw, not just a credibility prior)
confirms the skepticism was warranted. Logged per this project's Rule 11 standard: verified against
the primary source, not dismissed on reputation alone, and not accepted on reputation alone either.
