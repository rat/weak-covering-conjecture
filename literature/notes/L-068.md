## Why this was read

Retroactive triage of the E-004 search loop (2026-07-30), from the OpenAlex pass over the 141
works citing Tao. The original loop never opened it; the title alone reads like yet another
Collatz-adjacent note. It is not. Of everything the retroactive pass touched, this is the one
peer-reviewed paper whose central object is genuinely in the same arithmetic family as
`R_{j,k}`.

## The object

Following Akiyama, Frougny and Sakarovitch (Israel J. Math. 168 (2008) 53-91, indexed here as
L-069), every `n` in `N` has a unique representation

    n = (1/2) * sum_{i=0}^{k} a_i (3/2)^i,    a_i in {0,1,2},   a_k != 0 for n >= 1,

and the associated word `<n> = a_k a_{k-1} ... a_0` over the alphabet `{0,1,2}`. The language
`L = { <n> : n in N }` of admissible words is prefix-closed and, famously, not regular. The
length obeys `|<n>| = log_{3/2}(n) + O(1)`.

Clearing denominators makes the connection to this project visible:

    2^{k+1} n = sum_{i=0}^{k} a_i 3^i 2^{k-i}.

Compare `R_{j,k} = { sum_{i=0}^{j} 2^{alpha_i} 3^i : j+k >= alpha_0 > alpha_1 > ... > alpha_j >= 0 }`.
Both are sums of the shape `sum (digit) * 3^i * 2^(exponent)`. The two differ in which slot is
free. Here the exponent staircase is rigid (`k-i`, one step per index) and the digits `a_i` range
over `{0,1,2}`; in `R_{j,k}` the digits are all `1` and the exponents `alpha_i` are free subject
only to strict decrease and a budget cap.

That difference is exactly the one that matters for H-003. In the rational-base system the weight
`3^i` is attached to the fixed position `i`, an external index. In `R_{j,k}` the weight `3^i` is
attached to the `i`-th largest chosen exponent, i.e. to a rank. So this paper does not engage the
rank-coupling obstruction, and nothing in it bounds an exponential sum.

## What the paper proves

Two natural questions about `L`, both answered in terms of the Collatz map `T(n) = n/2` for even
`n`, `(3n+1)/2` for odd `n`:

1. Which of `w0`, `w1`, `w2` remain admissible when `w` is admissible (Section 3.1).
2. What is the lexicographically largest word of each length `k` in `L`, equivalently the largest
   `n` with `|<n>| = k` (Section 3.2). This is the sequence the authors call `sat(k)`, generated
   by iterating an odometer-type map `U`.

Concretely: for `n` odd, `<T(n)> = <n>1`. For `n` even the passage from `<n>` to `<T(n)>` is left
open (their Question 20). Their Proposition 21 gives the parity criterion

    n = 0 mod 2  <=>  sum_{i=0}^{k} 2^{k-i} 3^i a_i = 0 mod 2^{k+2},

a congruence on exactly the mixed-base sum above, but modulo a power of 2, not a power of 3.

Section 4 poses two conjectures the authors judge to be of Collatz-level difficulty: that the
proportion of digits `1` in `<sat(k)>` tends to `1/2` (Conjecture 18), and that the infinite limit
word `<sat(inf)>` is normal over `{1,2}` (Conjecture 19). Numerical support runs to `k = 2^20`,
where `<sat(2^20)>` has 525067 ones and 523509 twos and contains every word in `{1,2}^l` as a
subword for all `l < 17`.

## Verdict for this project

No technique to import, and no bearing on `S(t)`, `j*(l)`, or `e(l)`. Its value is as a citation
in both directions (Rule 10): it is recent, peer-reviewed, cites Tao's Forum Math. Pi paper, and
is written explicitly to attract further work on the mixed-base-2-and-3 representation of
integers, which is the arithmetic home of `R_{j,k}`. A paper on the Weak Covering Conjecture that
situates `R_{j,k}` against the rational-base `3/2` literature has a natural readership there and a
natural reason to be cited back.

Its reference list is also a compact map of that neighbourhood: Akiyama-Frougny-Sakarovitch 2008
(L-069), Morgenbesser-Steiner-Thuswaldner, "Patterns in rational base number systems", J. Fourier
Anal. Appl. 19 (2013) 225-250 (not obtained; the title suggests digit-pattern statistics in this
system and it is the obvious next thing to read if this direction is ever pursued), and Eliahou's
own 1993 lower bounds on nontrivial Collatz cycle lengths.
