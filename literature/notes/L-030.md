## The shared construction (same across all 8 papers)

Accelerated Collatz map `U(n) = (3n+1)/2^{v2(3n+1)}` on odd positive integers, valuation word
`a(n) = (a_0(n), a_1(n), ...)`, `a_j(n) = v2(3*U^j(n)+1)`, "residual debt"
`L_k(n) = k*log2(3) - sum_{j<k} a_j(n)`, dissipative potential `phi(a) = a_0 - log2(3)`. The
series studies this one object's statistics (central limit theorem, large deviations, Gibbs
measures, variational principle, spectral radius/pressure of a transfer operator built from
phi), a standard thermodynamic-formalism toolkit, applied consistently across all 8 papers,
not a scattered grab-bag of unrelated claims. Every abstract checked is explicit that the work
does not claim to prove the Collatz conjecture, and the transfer-operator paper's central
positive-eigenvalue statement is presented as **Conjetura de Perron-Frobenius Residual**
(Section 7), a labeled conjecture, not a claimed theorem.

## Credibility assessment

Read L-030 (013) in full: internally consistent notation across its own sections, propositions
build on each other correctly (checked several proofs, e.g. Proposition 1.1's well-definedness
argument and Proposition 2.1's induction, both correct and complete as stated), and the paper
is explicit and honest about what is proved (Propositions/Theorems 1.1-6.5, elementary and
correctly derived) versus what is conjectured (Section 7 onward, clearly labeled
"Conjetura"). This does **not** show the hallmarks of the low-credibility claimed-proof genre
already catalogued in this archive (`literature/INDEX.md` L-036): no leap from a conjecture to
"therefore Collatz is proved," no combining of unrelated famous open problems, no missing
justification for a load-bearing step in what's actually claimed as proven. Verdict: a
genuine, honestly-scoped exploratory research program building a thermodynamic-formalism
framework for the accelerated map's statistics, not proven to deliver anything yet (most of
its interesting consequences are explicitly conditional on the unproven Conjetura de
Perron-Frobenius Residual), and not a fabrication.

## Does L-030's transfer operator face H-003 round 6's S(t) reduction? Not applicable: it never addresses mod-3^l covering at all

Checked directly, not inferred from the abstract: searched the full extracted text of L-030
for "mod 3", "módulo 3", "cobertura"/"covering", "residuo" (in the modular-residue sense): **zero
hits** for any of these in a covering/residue-class sense (the one incidental match, `residuo`,
refers to the paper's own "normalized residue" term in its dissipation decomposition, unrelated
to residues mod 3^l). Confirmed the same null result across all 7 other papers in the series by
the same grep.

L-030's operator, `Definición 3.1`, acts on functions `f: Sigma_C -> R` where `Sigma_C` is the
space of infinite realizable valuation words (an infinite symbolic sequence space), via
preimages under the shift `sigma`, weighted by `e^{-t*phi(b)}`. This is a genuinely different
mathematical object from H-003 round 6's operator (`notes/H-003.md`, sixth section), which acts
on functions on the finite group `(Z/3^l Z)*` and translates "R_{j,k} covers mod 3^l" into a
statement about a specific coefficient of the operator's power series. Ruiz Castillo's spectral
radius / spectral pressure / Gibbs measures characterize long-run *statistical* behavior of the
accelerated map's symbolic dynamics as a whole (an ergodic-theory question about one infinite
system), not surjectivity of a finite, bounded-length family of sums onto every residue class
mod a specific 3^l (this project's actual question). The resemblance is at the level of
technique-name only ("transfer operator," "thermodynamic formalism"), not the same
mathematical target.

**Conclusion**: this is not "the same obstruction confirmed by an independent author," and it
is not "a different, more powerful transfer operator that evades the obstruction" either.
It's a different question, dressed in the same broad toolkit (which is unsurprising:
thermodynamic formalism is the standard tool for *any* study of a dynamical system's long-run
statistics, so its appearance here is not itself evidence of a deeper connection to H-003
specifically).

## A conceptual point worth flagging for H-001, not H-003

Ruiz Castillo's "residual debt" `L_k(n) = k*log2(3) - sum_{j<k} a_j(n)` and this project's own
`e(l) = j*(l) - l*log_4(3)` (`notes/H-001.md`) are both measuring deviation from a log-threshold
tied to the same 2-vs-3 growth-rate comparison (`log2(3) = 2*log4(3)`), in conceptually parallel
but not identical constructions (Ruiz Castillo's is a running sum along one infinite orbit;
this project's is about the minimal covering size of a specific finite combinatorial family).
Not a technical transfer, and not pursued further here, but worth noting as a "kindred
question" if a future session is looking for independent perspectives on why e(l) grows the
way it does (H-001), separate from H-003's specific bound-on-S(t) obstruction.

## Recommendation

No transferable technique found for H-003 (the series doesn't engage with the covering
question at all). Credibility is reasonably good for an unreviewed, self-published research
program (consistent, honestly labeled, no red flags of the claimed-proof genre) but nothing
here is a proven result usable as a citation for a strong claim; most of what would matter is
gated behind the unproven Perron-Frobenius conjecture in L-030. Worth the researcher's own
skim if the H-001 "kindred question" angle is ever pursued; not worth further H-003 effort.
