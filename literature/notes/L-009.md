# L-009: Monks, Monks, Monks & Monks, "Strongly sufficient sets and the distribution of arithmetic sequences in the 3x+1 graph"

arXiv:1204.3904v2 (math.DS), fetched and read in full via `pdftotext -layout` on the arXiv PDF,
2026-07-27.

## What the paper actually proves

Studies "sufficient sets" (S such that every positive integer's T-orbit merges with some
element of S, where merge means the forward orbits eventually coincide) and "strongly
sufficient sets" in the full 3x+1 dynamical graph (both T0: x->x/2 and T1: x->(3x+1)/2 moves
allowed, not just backward/inverse moves). Main results: sufficient sets of arbitrarily low
density exist; the group generated by T0, T1 acting mod b (b coprime to 6) is determined; every
orbit contains an element == 2 mod 9 and every non-trivial cycle/divergent orbit contains an
element == 20 mod 27 (Corollary 5.8, Theorem 6.4); a self-duality property of the 3x+1 digraph
mod 2^n (and only mod 2^n, proved not to hold for any other modulus, Theorem 7.1).

## Genuine technical connection found: Wirsching's back-tracing formalism, not R_{j,k} itself

Section 4 explicitly builds on Wirsching's book (cited as [12], same primary source as this
project's L-001) for its "back tracing function" formalism: a feasible vector
`s = (s_0,...,s_k) in F = union_k N^{k+1}` defines `v_s = T0^{-s_0} o T1^{-1} o ... o T0^{-s_k}`,
and **Wirsching's own theorem, used here, states that if l(s)=m>0 then there is a unique
congruence class a mod 3^m, coprime to 3, such that s is admissible for x iff x == a mod 3^m**
(p.6-7 of the PDF, right before Theorem 4.1). This is precisely a bijective correspondence
between length-m admissible back-tracing vectors and residues mod 3^m, which is structurally
close in spirit to how R_{j,k} enumerates residues via sequences of 2-power exponents. Worth
flagging for H-004 (this project's own hypothesis about Wirsching's 3-adic Markov/back-tracing
process): this paper is a concrete example of another author building directly on that same
formalism, giving a possible entry point (Wirsching's book itself, Chapter on back-tracing
functions, not yet read in this project past Chapter V Section 3) rather than something already
solved here.

**This is not the same object as R_{j,k}, and does not give a bound on j\*(l).** The back-tracing
vectors `v_s` here compose T0 and T1 moves through the *entire* 3x+1 graph (mixing division by 2
and the (3x+1)/2 step); R_{j,k} in this project's setting is a purely additive sum of terms
`2^{a_i} 3^i` with a specific decreasing-exponent constraint, arising from Wirsching's *inverse*
map alone applied j+1 times, not general back-tracing through the full graph. Theorem 4.1's
bound (`length <= 2(b-1)f + n + 1` for back-tracing to a residue mod `d = 2^n 3^m b`) is a
worst-case path-length bound for reaching an arbitrary target residue via arbitrary T0/T1 moves,
answering a different question ("how far is any x from hitting residue class a mod d") than j*(l)
("what is the smallest j such that the specific family R_{j-1,j} covers all of (Z/3^l Z)*"). No
formula, bound, or numeric value in this paper was found to translate into a bound on j*(l)
itself; did not attempt to force one.

## Relevance to H-005 (sufficient sets as an independent verification method)

Confirmed directly (not just from the abstract) that "sufficient set" here means orbit-merging
in the full forward/backward 3x+1 graph, a structurally different covering notion from
Wirsching's residue-covering (R_{j-1,j} covering all of (Z/3^l Z)* mod 3^l). No result in this
paper gives, or straightforwardly adapts into, an independent method to compute or bound j*(l).
H-005 as originally framed (a possible independent verification method) does not pan out from
this paper specifically; the more promising thread this paper actually surfaces is the shared
Wirsching back-tracing formalism (relevant to H-004, see above), not an alternative covering
notion useful for H-005's stated purpose.
