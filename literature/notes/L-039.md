# L-039: M. E. Spencer, "Rooted Surjectivity: An Invariant EO Refinement System"

Local archive, `literature/papers/037_Rooted-Surjectivity-Invariant-EO-Refinement-System.pdf`,
undated preprint, venue unclear. Same author as L-038. Read in full for H-007. By title, the
closest match found anywhere in this project's whole literature search to its own central
question (does a structured set surject onto/cover all residues).

## What it claims

A complete proof of "rooted inverse-surjectivity" (Theorem 15.1: R_1 = O, the set of odd integers
reachable by finite admissible inverse iteration from 1 equals all positive odd integers), and
therefore the full Collatz conjecture (Corollary 15.2).

## The specific, concrete flaw found

The whole argument depends on Theorem 5.3 ("Nontrivial acyclicity": no nontrivial cycle exists in
the odd inverse/forward system) as its first load-bearing step - everything downstream (the
normalized E/O phase system, gap propagation, exact finite-level occupancy) assumes acyclicity has
already been established.

Theorem 5.3's proof argues: a cycle requires *closure*, i.e. an integer `N` and word `K` with
`F_K(N) = N`, equivalent to `(2^S(K) - 3^|K|)N = B_K`. The paper's argument against this having a
solution is that the dyadic multiplier `2^S(K)` is "a unit modulo every power of 3", so "adjacent
or copied positions on a thread retain distinct terminal refinement phases at some finite depth,"
and concludes "the repeated word cannot repair a nonzero closure residual."

**This does not follow.** `gcd(2^S(K), 3^q) = 1` is true for *every* power of 2 and every `q`,
trivially (2 and 3 are coprime) - it says nothing about whether the specific Diophantine equation
`(2^S(K) - 3^|K|)N = B_K` has an integer solution for some particular `N`. Coprimality mod 3^q for
all q constrains residues, not the existence of an exact equality over the integers. Ruling out
that equation actually having a solution is precisely the hard, genuinely open part of proving no
nontrivial Collatz cycles exist (classically tied to Diophantine approximation results on the
irrationality measure of `log_2(3)`, e.g. bounds needed in Simons-de Weger-style cycle analyses).
The paper dispatches this with what amounts to a non-sequitur dressed in correct-sounding number
theory (the coprimality statement is true, just irrelevant to the conclusion drawn from it).

Everything downstream of Theorem 5.3 (the "gap propagation" argument in Sections 10-14, which
otherwise has a plausible proof shape - a real, finite-level covering count matching argument,
conceptually not unlike what this project itself does with `j*(l)`) inherits this unproven
foundation.

## Relevance to H-003

None found, same as L-038. No exponential sums, no character/Fourier machinery, no rank/position-
weighted combinatorial structure anywhere in the paper. The "exact dyadic valuation rails" (Sec.
6) and density bookkeeping (Sec. 12-13) are standard, correct 2-adic valuation facts already
well-known in this literature, not a new technique.

## Credibility verdict

Not credible as a full proof. Same author and same pattern as L-038 (`literature/notes/L-038.md`):
elaborate, formal-looking apparatus (Lean-adjacent notation, tables, a large dependency-chain
diagram) built around one unproven load-bearing step, dressed in language that sounds rigorous
without being so at the exact point that matters. A genuine solution to this specific,
famous, decades-open problem from an unreviewed single-author source remains an extraordinary
claim; this specific, checkable gap confirms rather than merely motivates the skepticism.
