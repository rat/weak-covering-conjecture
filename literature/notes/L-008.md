# L-008: A. Thomas, "A non-uniform distribution property of most orbits, in case the 3x+1 conjecture is true"

arXiv:1512.05852v2 (math.NT), fetched and read in full via `pdftotext -layout` on the arXiv PDF,
2026-07-27.

## What the paper actually proves

Defines `W` = the set of integers n whose forward orbit reaches 1 and is "approximately well
distributed" mod 18 among the six residues coprime to 6 (`{1,5,7,11,13,17}`), in a precise sense
bounding how far the empirical distribution of orbit elements across these six classes can be
from uniform. Main theorem: `W` has natural density 0, i.e. for almost all n, the orbit's
distribution among these six residue classes mod 18 is measurably non-uniform (some class is
visited disproportionately often). Proof method: analyzes back-tracing sequences via a transform
`S` (Sinai's version of the inverse Collatz map, `S(n) = (3n+1)/2^k`) and admissible-exponent
sequences `epsilon_1, epsilon_2, ...` constrained mod 6 by the current residue class mod 18
(equation 3, a 6-state transition table), then a large-deviations/generating-function argument
bounds the probability of near-uniform visitation.

## Direct check: no Wirsching or covering-conjecture connection found in the paper itself

Grepped the full text for "Wirsching", "covering", "R_{j,k}"-style notation, and "3^l"/"3^n"
(beyond the fixed modulus 18=2*3^2 used throughout): no hits. This paper does **not** cite
Wirsching, does not discuss the Weak Covering Conjecture, and does not use or reference the
`R_{j,k}` object. Tao's footnote (L-006) that names this paper alongside Wirsching's as both
being about "3-adic irregularities of the Collatz iteration" is accurate only at the level of
shared general subject matter (both study non-uniform 3-adic/mod-3^k structure in Collatz
dynamics), not a technical connection between the two papers' actual results or methods; Thomas
does not appear to have been aware of or building on Wirsching's covering conjecture specifically.

## Structural similarity noted, not a transferable result

The exponent sequence `epsilon_j` here (back-tracing via `n_{j+1} = (1/3)(2^{epsilon_{j+1}} n_j - 1)`,
equation 2) is structurally reminiscent of the exponent sequences underlying R_{j,k} (both are
built from repeated application of the inverse Collatz map with varying powers of 2), but the
paper's actual object of study is the *orbit-level distribution mod 18* of forward-iterated
sequences, not residue-covering mod 3^l by a specific structured sum family. No formula, bound,
or technique in this paper was found that transfers into a bound on j*(l) or R_{j,k}'s covering
behavior. Did not force a connection that isn't there.

## Verdict for H-004

This paper does not deliver on H-004's motivating question (whether Thomas's work gives an
independent derivation or cross-check of j*(l)/R_{j,k} covering behavior). It is topically
adjacent (3-adic Collatz structure) but methodologically and in its actual results, unconnected
to Wirsching's covering conjecture. H-004's more promising remaining thread, after this reading,
is the direct Wirsching-formalism connection found in L-009 (Monks et al.'s use of Wirsching's
own back-tracing function definitions), not this paper.
