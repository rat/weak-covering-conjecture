## What the paper actually is

Introduces "p-adic systems": piecewise functions on `Z_p` with one branch per residue class mod
`p`, whose resulting digit-expansion table satisfies a "block property" (the first `k` digits of
the expansions of `m` and `n` coincide iff `m == n mod p^k`). This is a general framework
unifying known number-system constructions (positional notation, canonical number systems, beta
expansions, shift radix systems) under one definitional umbrella, with results on: three
equivalent interpretations of the same object (functions with block property / digit tables with
block property / permutations of `Z_p` respecting congruence mod powers of `p`), a
characterization via independently-chosen branch functions, generalizations of Hensel's Lemma,
periodic/aperiodic digit-expansion theory for "linear-polynomial" p-adic systems, and permutation
polynomial cycle-tree structure. Genuinely Collatz-motivated (`F_C := (x, 3x+1)` on `Z_2` is the
paper's own running Section 7 example) and mathematically serious (FWF-funded, `p` need not even
be prime, careful theorem/proof structure throughout what was read).

## Relevance to H-003: none found, and the reason is structural, not superficial

**The paper's Collatz-adjacent question is periodicity of individual trajectories, not covering
by a structured finite family.** Section 7 ("Linear-polynomial p-adic systems and the question of
ultimate periodicity") studies, for a fixed p-adic system like `F_C=(x,3x+1)`, which p-adic
integers `n` have an eventually-periodic digit expansion under repeated application of `F_C`. For
`F_C` itself this is a direct restatement of the Collatz conjecture (every positive integer's
orbit is eventually periodic, entering the `(1,2)` cycle) in `p`-adic-dynamics language, not a
question about a fixed, explicitly-constructed finite subset of `Z_3` and whether it surjects
onto a residue group. The paper's own "Open questions and problems" (Section 9, read in full, 15
items) confirms this framing throughout: every item is about algorithmic properties of p-adic
system arithmetic, characterizing which functions are "(p,r)-suitable" or have the (weak) block
property, group structure of p-adic-system composition, generalized Hensel's Lemma applications,
permutation-polynomial cycle-tree structure, or ultimate-periodicity conjectures for specific
linear-polynomial families. None concerns bounding an exponential sum over a rank-coupled index
set, or proving/disproving that a structured combinatorial family covers a residue group -- the
two things H-003 actually needs.

**The "block property" is a different, more general condition than the "block-separated"
construction Codex used.** This paper's block property is about *any* p-adic system's digit
table being self-consistent with congruence (a structural well-formedness condition satisfied by
essentially every reasonable digit-expansion scheme, proved to hold for `F_C` and `F_2` in the
paper's own opening example). Codex's construction that proved `j*(l)<=3l-2` groups *exponents*
into disjoint blocks within a single covering construction for one fixed modulus `3^l` -- a
different, narrower, one-off combinatorial device, not an instance of this paper's general
framework. The vocabulary overlap ("block") is coincidental, not a shared technical result.

## Verdict

Real paper, correctly identified as topically adjacent (p-adic, Collatz-motivated, digit-block
structure), but **not applicable to H-003's actual obstruction** after a substantial read, for a
structural reason (individual-orbit periodicity vs. finite-family covering), not a superficial
one. Consistent with, not contradicting, every other literature search this project has run on
H-003: the closest published match remains T. Cochrane's exponential-sum work (L-019/L-020),
itself already found not to reach the rank-coupled index domain. No further action on this paper
planned; the unread ~70 pages (general polynomial/rational p-adic system theory, Hensel's Lemma
generalizations, permutation cycle trees) are judged low-probability of changing this verdict
given Section 9's own complete list of what the paper considers its open directions, none of which
touches covering/exponential-sum questions.
